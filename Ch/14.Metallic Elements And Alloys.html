Metallic Elements & Alloys

This chapter covers the properties of metals, the concept of alloys, the Reactivity Series, the extraction of iron in the Blast Furnace, and methods to prevent corrosion.

1. Properties of Metals

Metals are located on the left side of the Periodic Table. Their physical properties are determined by their structure.

  • Conductivity: They conduct heat and electricity because they have a "sea" of delocalized electrons that are free to move.
  • Malleability & Ductility: They can be bent and drawn into wires because the layers of positive ions can slide over each other without breaking the metallic bond.
  • Structure: A regular lattice of positive ions surrounded by a sea of delocalized electrons.

2. Alloys

An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements (metals or non-metals). Alloys are generally harder and stronger than pure metals.

Why are alloys harder?
In an alloy, atoms of different sizes are present. These different sizes disrupt the regular arrangement of the layers, making it difficult for the layers to slide over each other.

Common Alloys:

  • Brass: Copper + Zinc (Musical instruments, pins).
  • Stainless Steel: Iron + Chromium + Nickel (Cutlery, surgical instruments - does not rust).

Displacement Reactions

A more reactive metal will displace (push out) a less reactive metal from its compound (oxide or salt solution).

  • $Mg + CuSO_4 \rightarrow MgSO_4 + Cu$ (Magnesium is more reactive than Copper).
  • $Fe + Al_2O_3 \rightarrow$ No Reaction (Iron is less reactive than Aluminum).
  • Thermite Reaction: Aluminum powder + Iron(III) Oxide. Highly exothermic. Used to weld rails. $2Al + Fe_2O_3 \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + 2Fe$.

4. Extraction of Iron (Blast Furnace)

Iron is extracted from Hematite ($Fe_2O_3$) using Carbon (Coke) in a Blast Furnace.

Raw Materials:

  1. Hematite: Source of iron.
  2. Coke (Carbon): Fuel and reducing agent.
  3. Limestone ($CaCO_3$): Removes acidic impurities (sand/silica).
  4. Hot Air: Provides Oxygen.
Key Equations in the Blast Furnace:
  1. Heat Generation: $C + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2$ (Exothermic).
  2. Forming Reducing Agent: $CO_2 + C \rightarrow 2CO$.
  3. Reduction of Iron Ore: $Fe_2O_3 + 3CO \rightarrow 2Fe + 3CO_2$.
  4. Slag Formation:
    $CaCO_3 \rightarrow CaO + CO_2$ (Thermal Decomposition)
    $CaO + SiO_2 \rightarrow CaSiO_3$ (Calcium Silicate / Slag).
Slag floats on top of molten iron and is used for road building.

5. Extraction of Zinc

Zinc is extracted from Zinc Blende ($ZnS$).

  1. Roasting: $2ZnS + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2ZnO + 2SO_2$.
  2. Reduction: $ZnO + C \rightarrow Zn + CO$. Zinc forms as a gas and condenses.

6. Corrosion of Iron (Rusting)

Rusting is the corrosion of iron and steel. It requires both Oxygen and Water.

Prevention Methods:

  • Barrier Methods: Painting, Greasing, Plastic coating. (Stops $O_2$/$H_2O$ reaching iron).
  • Galvanizing: Coating iron with Zinc. Zinc acts as a barrier AND provides sacrificial protection.
  • Sacrificial Protection: Attaching a block of a more reactive metal (like Magnesium or Zinc) to the iron. The more reactive metal corrodes instead of the iron.
