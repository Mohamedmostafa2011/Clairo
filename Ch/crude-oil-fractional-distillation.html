01 — Origin

Crude Oil Formation

🦠
Source
Ancient Organisms
Crude oil forms from the remains of dead plants and animals — mainly marine plankton — buried deep underground over millions of years.
Fossil Fuel
🌡️
Conditions
Heat + Pressure
Over millions of years, high temperatures and enormous pressure underground transform buried organic matter into crude oil (petroleum).
⏳
Timescale
Millions of Years
The formation process takes so long that at our current extraction rate, oil will eventually run out completely — long before it can naturally regenerate.
🚫
Availability
Non-Renewable
Crude oil is a finite resource. Once used, it cannot be replenished within a human timescale, making conservation and alternatives critical.
Non-Renewable

02 — Process

How Distillation Works

01
Heating the Crude Oil
Crude oil is heated to very high temperatures until the majority of it vaporises and turns into a gas mixture ready for separation.
02
Entering the Fractionating Column
The hot gas mixture enters a tall fractionating column — very hot at the bottom and gradually cooler at the top.
03
Rising & Cooling
As gases rise through the column, they cool down. Each hydrocarbon condenses back into liquid when it reaches a temperature just below its own boiling point.
04
Long Chains Condense First
Longer hydrocarbon chains have higher boiling points — they condense quickly and collect near the bottom of the column.
05
Short Chains Rise Higher
Shorter hydrocarbon chains have lower boiling points — they travel higher before condensing and are collected near the top.
06
LPG Remains as Gas
Some hydrocarbons like LPG (propane & butane) have extremely low boiling points — they remain as gas throughout the process and are collected at the very top.

04 — Properties

Key Properties of Fractions

🔥
Flammability
Shorter chains at the top are more flammable — they ignite easily, making them better fuels.
💧
Viscosity
Longer chains at the bottom are thicker and more viscous — harder to burn, better as lubricants.
🌡️
Boiling Point
Longer chains have higher boiling points. Shorter chains have lower boiling points and condense higher in the column.
⚙️
Cracking
Long-chain fractions that are poor fuels can be cracked — broken into smaller, more useful hydrocarbons.
