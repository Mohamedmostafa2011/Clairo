Intro to Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon-based compounds. Carbon is unique because it can form four covalent bonds, allowing it to create long chains and rings, forming millions of different compounds.

1. Naming Organic Compounds

The name of an organic molecule tells you two things: how many carbon atoms it has (the prefix) and what family it belongs to (the suffix).

A. The Prefixes (Number of Carbons)

You must memorise the prefixes for the first four carbon chains.

Monkeys Eat Peeled Bananas
Meth- (1 Carbon)
Eth- (2 Carbons)
Prop- (3 Carbons)
But- (4 Carbons)

B. The Suffixes (Homologous Series)

  • -ane: Alkane (C-C single bonds) e.g., Ethane
  • -ene: Alkene (C=C double bond) e.g., Ethene
  • -ol: Alcohol (-OH group) e.g., Ethanol
  • -oic acid: Carboxylic Acid (-COOH group) e.g., Ethanoic Acid

2. Homologous Series

A homologous series is a "family" of organic compounds that have the same functional group.

Characteristics of a Homologous Series
  • Same General Formula.
  • Same Functional Group (the part that reacts).
  • Similar Chemical Properties (they react in the same way).
  • Trend in Physical Properties (e.g., boiling point increases as chain length increases).
Family Functional Group General Formula Example
Alkanes None (Single bonds) CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ Methane (CH₄)
Alkenes C=C Double Bond CₙH₂ₙ Ethene (C₂H₄)
Alcohols -OH (Hydroxyl) CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)
Carboxylic Acids -COOH CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH Ethanoic Acid (CH₃COOH)

3. Structural Isomerism

Isomers are molecules with the same molecular formula but a different structural formula.

Example: C₄H₁₀ (Butane)
1. Straight chain: All 4 carbons in a row (Butane).
2. Branched chain: 3 carbons in a row, with 1 carbon branching off the middle (2-methylpropane).

Tip: Make sure you can draw both "straight" and "branched" chains.

4. Fuels & Fractional Distillation

Petroleum (Crude Oil) is a mixture of hydrocarbons. It is not useful until separated into fractions based on boiling points.

How it works:

  • Crude oil is heated and vaporised.
  • It enters a fractionating column (Hot at bottom, Cool at top).
  • Vapors rise and condense at different heights depending on their boiling point.
  • Small molecules (low boiling point) rise to the top. Large molecules (high boiling point) stay at the bottom.
Fraction Name Use Molecule Size
Refinery Gas Heating and cooking (Bottled gas) Smallest (Top)
Gasoline (Petrol) Fuel for cars Small
Naphtha Making chemicals (Feedstock) Medium
Kerosene Jet fuel (Aircraft) Medium
Diesel Fuel for trucks/trains (Diesel engines) Large
Fuel Oil Fuel for ships and home heating Large
Bitumen Surfacing roads and roofs Largest (Bottom)

5. Saturated vs Unsaturated

Saturated (Alkanes)

Contains only single C-C bonds. Has the maximum number of hydrogens possible.

Unsaturated (Alkenes)

Contains at least one C=C double bond. Can accept more atoms (addition reactions).

The Bromine Water Test

This is the definitive test to distinguish Alkanes from Alkenes.

  • Add Bromine Water (Orange) to the substance.
  • Alkane: No change (Solution stays Orange).
  • Alkene: Solution turns Colorless (Decolourised).
