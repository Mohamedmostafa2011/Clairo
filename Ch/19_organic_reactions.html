Reactions of Organic Compounds

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon-containing compounds. This chapter covers the key reactions of the main homologous series: alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, and carboxylic acids.

1. Key Definitions

TermDefinition
Homologous SeriesA family of organic compounds with the same general formula and functional group, each differing by CH2.
Functional GroupThe atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.
SaturatedContains only C–C single bonds (e.g. Alkanes). Cannot undergo addition.
UnsaturatedContains at least one C=C double bond (e.g. Alkenes). Can undergo addition reactions.
IsomersCompounds with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements.

2. Alkanes

General formula: CnH2n+2. Saturated hydrocarbons. Relatively unreactive due to strong C–H and C–C bonds.

NameFormulaState (RTP)
MethaneCH4Gas
EthaneC2H6Gas
PropaneC3H8Gas
ButaneC4H10Gas

Combustion of Alkanes

Complete combustion (excess oxygen) produces CO2 and H2O:

CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O

Incomplete combustion (limited oxygen) produces CO (toxic) and/or carbon (soot):

2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO + 4H2O

Substitution Reaction (with Halogens)

Alkanes react with halogens (e.g. Cl2) in the presence of UV light — a photochemical reaction. One H is replaced by a halogen atom.

CH4 + Cl2 ⟶ [UV light] CH3Cl + HCl
Why UV light?
The energy from UV radiation is needed to break the Cl–Cl bond and start the reaction (initiation step). Without UV light, the reaction will NOT occur.

3. Alkenes

General formula: CnH2n. Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing a C=C double bond. Much more reactive than alkanes.

Test for Alkenes: Bromine water goes from Orange/Brown → Colourless (decolourised).

Addition Reactions

The C=C double bond opens up and atoms add across it. No atoms are lost — all reactants become products.

ReactionReagentConditionsProduct
HydrogenationH2Ni catalyst, 150°CAlkane (e.g. Ethane)
HalogenationBr2 (bromine water)Room tempDibromoalkane (colourless)
HydrationH2O (steam)H3PO4 catalyst, 300°C, 60atmAlcohol (e.g. Ethanol)
HydrohalogenationHBrRoom tempBromoalkane

Equations for Ethene (C2H4)

C2H4 + H2 → C2H6  (Hydrogenation)
C2H4 + Br2 → CH2BrCH2Br  (Halogenation)
C2H4 + H2O → C2H5OH  (Hydration)

Polymerisation (Addition Polymerisation)

Many small alkene molecules (monomers) join together to form a long chain polymer. The C=C double bond opens up.

n(CH2=CH2) → –(CH2–CH2)–n  (Poly(ethene))
Addition vs Substitution
Addition: Two reactants → ONE product. Nothing removed. Requires C=C. (Alkenes)
Substitution: One atom replaced by another. Two products formed. (Alkanes + halogen with UV)

4. Alcohols

General formula: CnH2n+1OH. Functional group: –OH (hydroxyl group). Ethanol (C2H5OH) is the most important.

ReactionEquation / ConditionsProducts
CombustionBurns in O2CO2 + H2O
OxidationOxidising agent (e.g. acidified K2Cr2O7)Carboxylic Acid
DehydrationAl2O3 catalyst, heatAlkene + Water
EsterificationCarboxylic acid + conc. H2SO4, heatEster + Water

Combustion of Ethanol

C2H5OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O

Dehydration of Ethanol

Removes water from ethanol to form ethene (the reverse of hydration):

C2H5OH ⟶ [Al2O3, heat] C2H4 + H2O

5. Carboxylic Acids

General formula: CnH2n+1COOH. Functional group: –COOH (carboxyl group). They are weak acids — only partially dissociate in water.

ReactionReagentsProducts
With metalsMg, Zn, FeSalt + H2 gas
With carbonatesNa2CO3, CaCO3Salt + H2O + CO2
With basesNaOH, KOHSalt + H2O (neutralisation)
EsterificationAlcohol + conc. H2SO4, heatEster + Water

Esterification

A carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol in the presence of a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst to form an ester and water. This is a reversible reaction.

CH3COOH + C2H5OH ⇌ CH3COOC2H5 + H2O

(Ethanoic acid + Ethanol → Ethyl ethanoate + Water)

Naming Esters
Name = [alcohol part]-yl + [acid part]-anoate
Ethanol + Ethanoic acid → Ethyl ethanoate
Methanol + Ethanoic acid → Methyl ethanoate
Ethanol + Propanoic acid → Ethyl propanoate
