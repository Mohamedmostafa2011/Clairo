Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry is the study of the production of electricity from chemical reactions or the use of electricity to cause chemical changes (Electrolysis).

1. Key Definitions & Principles

TermDefinition
ElectrolysisThe breakdown of an ionic compound, molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of electricity.
ElectrolyteThe compound that conducts electricity when molten or dissolved (contains free-moving ions).
ElectrodeRod (usually Graphite or Platinum) that carries current into the liquid.
PANIC: Positive Anode, Negative Is Cathode
Electron Flow & Redox
Anode (+): Negative ions (Anions) are attracted here. They lose electrons (Oxidation).
Cathode (-): Positive ions (Cations) are attracted here. They gain electrons (Reduction).

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

2. Electrolysis of Molten Compounds

In molten compounds, there are only two types of ions. It is simple: the metal goes to the cathode, and the non-metal goes to the anode.

Example: Molten Lead(II) Bromide (PbBr2)

  • Ions Present: Pb2+ and Br−
  • Cathode (-): Pb2+ gains electrons to become Lead metal (Grey bead).
    Pb2+ + 2e− → Pb
  • Anode (+): Br− loses electrons to become Bromine gas (Red-brown gas).
    2Br− → Br2 + 2e−

3. Electrolysis of Aqueous Solutions

Aqueous solutions contain the ions from the salt PLUS ions from water: H+ and OH−. We must follow the rules of selective discharge.

The Rules of Discharge

At the Cathode (-):
Usually, the less reactive element is discharged.
  • If metal is more reactive than Hydrogen (e.g., Na, K, Ca, Mg), Hydrogen gas is produced (2H+ + 2e− → H2).
  • If metal is less reactive than Hydrogen (e.g., Cu, Ag), the Metal is produced.

At the Anode (+):
  • If Halide ions are present (Cl−, Br−, I−) in concentration, the Halogen is produced.
  • If no Halide (e.g., Sulfates, Nitrates), Oxygen is produced from Hydroxide ions.
    4OH− → O2 + 2H2O + 4e−

Example 1: Concentrated Aqueous NaCl (Brine)

  • Ions: Na+, Cl−, H+, OH−
  • Cathode: H+ discharged (Na is too reactive). Product: Hydrogen Gas.
  • Anode: Cl− discharged (Halide rule). Product: Chlorine Gas.
  • Remaining Solution: Na+ and OH− remain → Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH).

Example 2: Dilute Sulfuric Acid (H2SO4)

  • Cathode: H+ → Hydrogen gas.
  • Anode: OH− → Oxygen gas (Sulfate SO42- never discharges).

5. Chemical Cells & Fuel Cells

Simple Cell: Two metals of different reactivity in an electrolyte create a voltage. The further apart in the reactivity series, the higher the voltage.

Electron flow: From More Reactive Metal → Less Reactive Metal.

Hydrogen Fuel Cells

Combines Hydrogen and Oxygen to produce electricity without combustion.

  • Fuel: Hydrogen (H2) and Oxygen (O2).
  • Product: Only Water (H2O). Non-polluting.
  • Overall Equation:
    2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
