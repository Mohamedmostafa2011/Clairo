Organic Chemistry

  • Defined as the study of carbon-based compounds. 0:21
  • Carbon is versatile, forming four strong bonds with other carbon or hydrogen atoms. 0:32
  • Hydrocarbons are formed only from carbon and hydrogen. 0:53
  • Example: Butane is a hydrocarbon; Butanol is not (contains oxygen). 1:02

The Alkane Series

  • Alkanes are the simplest type of hydrocarbons. 1:17
  • The first four alkanes are:
    1. Methane 1:26
    2. Ethane 1:37
    3. Propane 1:43
    4. Butane 1:48
  • They form a homologous series, meaning they share similar properties and reaction types. 2:05

The General Formula

  • The formula for alkanes is:
    CnH2n+2 2:23
  • This allows you to determine the molecular formula of any alkane.
  • Examples:
    Propane: C3H8 2:42
    Octane: C8H18 3:15

Saturated Compounds

  • Alkanes are classified as saturated compounds. 3:47
  • This means they contain only single covalent bonds between carbon atoms. 3:51
  • Comparison: Saturated alkanes vs. Unsaturated alkenes (which contain double bonds). 3:59
