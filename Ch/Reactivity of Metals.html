Reactive Metals

Understanding chemical behavior & extraction methods

Simple Advanced
01
💧
K, Na, Ca

React with Cold Water

These are very reactive metals. When they touch cold water, they grab the hydroxide (OH⁻) directly.

Reaction Sodium + Water → Sodium Hydroxide + Hydrogen

These metals are top of the reactivity series. They donate electrons violently. Even if an oxide forms, water converts it:

Mechanism Metal Oxide + H₂O → Metal Hydroxide
  • Why: Water contains H⁺ and OH⁻.
  • Result: Stable alkaline solution.
02
♨️
Mg, Al, Fe, Sn

React with Steam

Cold water is too weak. Steam (hot water gas) splits into H and O atoms. The metal grabs the Oxygen to form an Oxide.

Reaction Mg + Steam → Magnesium Oxide + Hydrogen

Thermal decomposition prevents hydroxides from forming. Steam provides energy to break the metal's initial oxide layer.

Decomposition M(OH)₂ + Heat → Metal Oxide + H₂O
  • Key: High temps favor Oxide stability.
  • Kinetic Energy: Breaks bonds in water molecule.
03
🛡️
Pb (Lead)

The Lead Anomaly

Lead should react, but it doesn't. It forms a protective "skin" (oxide layer) that blocks water and acid from touching the metal underneath.

This is called Passivation. Lead forms a dense, insoluble layer of Lead(II) oxide (PbO).

  • Barrier: The layer is tightly packed.
  • Insoluble: Water/Acids cannot penetrate.
  • Result: Reaction stops immediately after starting.
04
🔥
Extraction

Reduction with Carbon

Used for medium-reactive metals (Iron, Zinc). We heat the ore with Carbon (Coke). Carbon steals the oxygen, leaving pure metal.

Example Iron Oxide + Carbon → Iron + CO

Carbon acts as a Reducing Agent. At high temps (Blast Furnace), Carbon has a higher affinity for oxygen than the metal does.

  • Cost: Very cheap and efficient.
  • Chemistry: Redox reaction (Metal is reduced, Carbon is oxidized).
05
⚡
Extraction

Electrolysis

For super reactive metals (Al, Na). Carbon isn't strong enough. We melt the ore and use strong electricity to rip the elements apart.

Process Electricity splits Molten Ionic Compound

Used when the bond between Metal and Oxygen is too strong for Carbon. Requires huge energy to maintain molten state.

  • Cathode (-): Metal ions gain electrons (Reduction).
  • Anode (+): Non-metal ions lose electrons (Oxidation).
  • Note: Used for anything above Carbon in reactivity series.
