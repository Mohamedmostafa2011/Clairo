Acids and Bases

This topic explores the chemistry of protons. You must understand the definitions, the pH scale, the distinction between strong and weak acids, and the three major chemical reactions (MASH, BAWS, CAWCS).

1. Precise Definitions

In IGCSE Chemistry, we define acids and bases by their ability to transfer protons (Hydrogen ions, H+).

1. Acid (The Proton Donor) An acid is a substance that dissolves in water to produce hydrogen ions (H+).
In terms of proton transfer: An acid is a proton donor.
HCl(aq) → H+(aq) + Cl-(aq)
2. Base (The Proton Acceptor) A base is a substance that neutralises an acid. Bases are usually metal oxides or metal hydroxides.
In terms of proton transfer: A base is a proton acceptor.
NH3(aq) + H+(aq) → NH4+(aq)
3. Alkali (The Soluble Base) An alkali is a special type of base that is soluble in water. Alkalis dissolve to produce hydroxide ions (OH-).
NaOH(aq) → Na+(aq) + OH-(aq)
Crucial Detail: The Role of Water
Acids only display acidic properties when dissolved in water. For example, hydrogen chloride gas (HCl gas) is not acidic. It must dissolve in water to dissociate into H+ and Cl- ions to behave as an acid.

2. Strong vs. Weak Acids

The terms "strong" and "weak" refer to the extent of ionization (dissociation) in water, not the concentration.

Strong Acids

A strong acid fully ionizes in aqueous solution. Every single molecule breaks apart into ions.

  • Hydrochloric Acid: HCl → H+ + Cl-
  • Sulfuric Acid: H2SO4 → 2H+ + SO42-
  • Nitric Acid: HNO3 → H+ + NO3-

Weak Acids

A weak acid partially ionizes in aqueous solution. Most molecules stay intact; only a few release protons. We use a reversible arrow (⇌) to show this.

  • Ethanoic Acid: CH3COOH ⇌ H+ + CH3COO-

3. The pH Scale & Indicators

The pH scale measures the acidity or alkalinity of a solution. It runs from 0 to 14.

  • pH < 7: Acidic (Excess H+ ions)
  • pH = 7: Neutral (H+ equals OH-)
  • pH > 7: Alkaline (Excess OH- ions)

Exam Tip: Soil Acidity
Most plants grow best at pH 7. If soil is too acidic (pH < 6), farmers treat it with Quicklime (Calcium Oxide) or Slaked Lime (Calcium Hydroxide). These are bases that neutralise the acid in the soil.

4. Chemical Reactions (The Mnemonics)

You must memorize these three general reactions. Use the mnemonics MASH, BAWS, and CAWCS.

M A S H
Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen

Acids react with metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series.

  • Example: Magnesium + Sulfuric Acid → Magnesium Sulfate + Hydrogen
  • Equation: Mg(s) + H2SO4(aq) → MgSO4(aq) + H2(g)
  • Observations: Rapid effervescence (bubbling), metal dissolves, heat released (exothermic).
  • Test for H2: A lighted splint makes a "squeaky pop" sound.
B A W S
Base + Acid → Water + Salt

This is a Neutralisation reaction. Bases include Metal Oxides and Metal Hydroxides.

  • Example 1: Copper(II) Oxide + Hydrochloric Acid → Copper(II) Chloride + Water
  • Equation: CuO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CuCl2(aq) + H2O(l)
  • Ionic Equation (for soluble bases): H+(aq) + OH-(aq) → H2O(l)
C A W C S
Carbonate + Acid → Water + Carbon Dioxide + Salt
  • Example: Calcium Carbonate + Nitric Acid → Calcium Nitrate + Water + Carbon Dioxide
  • Equation: CaCO3(s) + 2HNO3(aq) → Ca(NO3)2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)
  • Observations: Effervescence (fizzing), solid dissolves.
  • Test for CO2: Bubble the gas through limewater (calcium hydroxide); it turns cloudy/milky.

Special Case: Ammonium Salts

Ammonium salts react with strong bases to release Ammonia gas.

  • General Eq: Ammonium Salt + Base → Salt + Water + Ammonia
  • Example: NH4Cl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O + NH3
  • Observation: Pungent smell of ammonia.
  • Test for Ammonia: Damp red litmus paper turns blue.

5. Types of Oxides

Oxides are classified by their reaction behavior. This is a frequent topic in Paper 2 (Multiple Choice).

A. Acidic Oxides

These are oxides of Non-Metals. They react with bases to form salts.

  • Examples: Carbon Dioxide (CO2), Sulfur Dioxide (SO2), Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2).
  • Property: They often dissolve in water to form acids (e.g., SO2 + H2O → H2SO3, sulfurous acid).

B. Basic Oxides

These are oxides of Metals. They react with acids to form salts.

  • Examples: Copper(II) Oxide (CuO), Magnesium Oxide (MgO), Calcium Oxide (CaO).
  • Property: If soluble, they form alkalis (e.g., Na2O + H2O → 2NaOH).

C. Amphoteric Oxides (Memorize These!)

Amphoteric oxides are chemically "two-faced." They can behave as both an acid and a base.

  • React with Acid: ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2O
  • React with Alkali: ZnO + 2NaOH → Na2ZnO2 (Sodium Zincate) + H2O
  • Examples: Aluminium Oxide (Al2O3) and Zinc Oxide (ZnO).

D. Neutral Oxides

These react with neither acids nor bases.

  • Examples: Water (H2O), Carbon Monoxide (CO), Nitrogen Monoxide (NO).
