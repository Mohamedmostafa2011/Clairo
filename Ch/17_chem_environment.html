Chemistry of Our Environment

This chapter studies Earth's atmosphere, the threats it faces from human activity, water chemistry, and how industrial processes interact with the natural world.

1. The Atmosphere

The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding Earth. Its composition has remained roughly stable for the past 200 million years — until industrialisation.

2. Air Pollution & Pollutant Gases

Burning fossil fuels releases gases that pollute the atmosphere and cause serious environmental problems.

The Main Pollutant Gases:

Carbon Monoxide (CO) — from incomplete combustion of carbon fuels.
Sulfur Dioxide (SO2) — from burning sulfur-containing fuels (coal, oil).
Oxides of Nitrogen (NO, NO2) — formed at high temperatures in car engines.
Carbon Dioxide (CO2) — from complete combustion; the main greenhouse gas.
Methane (CH4) — from decomposition of organic matter, cattle, rice paddies.

Carbon Monoxide (CO)

  • Source: Incomplete combustion when there is limited Oxygen.
    2C + O2 → 2CO
  • Problem: Colourless, odourless — very toxic gas. Binds to haemoglobin in blood, blocking Oxygen transport. Can be fatal.

Sulfur Dioxide (SO2) & Acid Rain

  • Source: Burning fossil fuels containing Sulfur impurities.
    S + O2 → SO2
  • Acid Rain Formation: SO2 reacts with water and Oxygen in the atmosphere:
    2SO2 + O2 + 2H2O → 2H2SO4
  • Effects of Acid Rain:
    • Damages and kills trees and vegetation.
    • Acidifies lakes and rivers — kills aquatic life.
    • Corrodes limestone buildings and statues.
    • Corrodes metals in structures and bridges.
  • Reducing SO2: Use low-sulfur fuels; scrub waste gases with Calcium Oxide (CaO).

Oxides of Nitrogen (NOx)

  • Source: Car engines and power stations — very high temperatures cause N2 and O2 in air to react.
    N2 + O2 → 2NO
  • NO then reacts with more Oxygen to form NO2, which dissolves in rain to form nitric acid (HNO3) — also contributing to Acid Rain.
  • Reduction: Catalytic converters in cars convert NOx to harmless N2.
    2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2

3. The Greenhouse Effect & Climate Change

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that keeps Earth warm enough to support life. Problems arise when human activity enhances this effect.

How the Greenhouse Effect Works:

1. The Sun emits short-wave radiation that passes through the atmosphere and warms Earth's surface.
2. Earth re-emits long-wave (infrared) radiation.
3. Greenhouse gases absorb this infrared radiation and re-radiate it back to Earth — trapping heat.
4. The more greenhouse gases → more heat trapped → global temperatures rise.

Greenhouse Gases

GasMain Human Source
Carbon Dioxide (CO2)Burning fossil fuels, deforestation
Methane (CH4)Livestock, landfill, rice cultivation
Water Vapour (H2O)Natural; amplified by warming
Nitrous Oxide (N2O)Fertilisers, agriculture

Consequences of Enhanced Greenhouse Effect

  • Rising average global temperatures (Global Warming).
  • Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers → rising sea levels.
  • Flooding of low-lying coastal areas.
  • More extreme weather events (droughts, storms, floods).
  • Disruption to ecosystems and loss of biodiversity.
  • Changed patterns of crop growth — threat to food security.

Reducing Greenhouse Gas Emissions

  • Switch to renewable energy (solar, wind, hydroelectric).
  • Improve energy efficiency (insulation, electric cars).
  • Reforestation — trees absorb CO2 via photosynthesis.
  • Reduce use of fossil fuels (coal, oil, natural gas).
  • Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology.

4. The Ozone Layer

The ozone layer is found in the stratosphere, about 15–40 km above Earth's surface. It absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun.

Ozone (O3) Formation and Depletion:

Formation: O2 absorbs UV radiation and splits; atomic oxygen combines with O2:
O2 → 2O (UV)  then  O + O2 → O3

Destruction by CFCs: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) — from old aerosols and fridges — release Chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy ozone:
Cl + O3 → ClO + O2    then    ClO + O → Cl + O2

(One Cl atom can destroy ~100,000 O3 molecules — it is a catalyst!)
  • Effects of ozone depletion: More UV reaches Earth's surface → increased rates of skin cancer, cataracts, damage to marine ecosystems.
  • Solution: CFCs have been banned under the Montreal Protocol (1987) and replaced with safer HFCs.

5. Water

Water is an essential resource. Understanding its chemistry — purity, treatment, and safe supply — is a key part of environmental chemistry.

Water Purity

  • Pure water contains only H2O molecules. It boils at 100°C and freezes at 0°C.
  • Natural water contains dissolved salts, minerals, and bacteria — it is not chemically pure.
  • Dissolved ions (e.g. Ca2+, Mg2+) make water hard.

Water Treatment (Potable Water)

StepProcessPurpose
1Sedimentation / FiltrationRemove large solid particles and mud.
2Coagulation / FlocculationAluminium sulfate added to clump fine particles together.
3Sand FiltrationSlow sand filter removes finer particles.
4ChlorinationChlorine gas added to kill harmful bacteria and micro-organisms.
SFSC: Settle → Filter → Sand → Chlorinate

Hard Water

  • Caused by dissolved Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions from limestone/chalk rocks.
  • Problem: Does not lather well with soap; forms scum. Causes limescale in boilers and kettles (wastes energy).
  • Temporary Hardness — caused by Ca(HCO3)2. Removed by boiling:
    Ca(HCO3)2 → CaCO3 + H2O + CO2
  • Permanent Hardness — caused by CaSO4. Cannot be removed by boiling. Removed by adding Na2CO3 (washing soda) which precipitates Ca2+.
  • Ion Exchange Resins — remove both types of hardness by exchanging Ca2+/Mg2+ ions for Na+ or H+.

Water Pollution

  • Sewage: Contains harmful bacteria and pathogens → treated in sewage works.
  • Fertiliser run-off (Eutrophication): Nitrates/phosphates from farms wash into rivers → algae blooms → algae die → bacteria decompose them → bacteria use up Oxygen → fish and aquatic life die.
  • Heavy metals (e.g. lead from old pipes) — toxic even in small concentrations.
  • Oil spills — harm marine wildlife; difficult to clean up.
