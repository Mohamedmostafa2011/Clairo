Formulae & Equations

Stoichiometry is the branch of chemistry that deals with the quantitative relationships between the reactants and products in a chemical reaction. This topic covers the Mole concept, balancing equations, and calculating yields.

1. Formulas and Balancing

A chemical formula tells you the ratio of atoms of each element in a compound. In a balanced equation, the number of atoms of each element is the same on both the reactant and product sides (Law of Conservation of Mass).

Steps to Balance Equations:
  1. Write the correct formula for every reactant and product.
  2. Count atoms of each element on both sides.
  3. Add big numbers (coefficients) in front of formulas to balance. Never change the small subscript numbers!

2. Relative Masses (Ar and Mr)

Since atoms are too small to weigh individually, we use a relative scale based on Carbon-12.

  • Relative Atomic Mass (Ar): The average mass of naturally occurring atoms of an element compared to 1/12th of a Carbon-12 atom. (Found on Periodic Table).
  • Relative Molecular Mass (Mr): The sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule.

3. The Mole Concept

The mole is the unit for the amount of substance. One mole contains Avogadro's Constant number of particles.

Avogadro's Constant:
6.02 × 1023 This is the number of atoms in exactly 12g of Carbon-12.

Key Calculation: Mass and Moles

This is the most important formula in stoichiometry:

Moles = Mass (g) / Mr Or rearranged: Mass = Moles × Mr

4. Molar Volume of Gases

At Room Temperature and Pressure (RTP), one mole of any gas occupies the same volume.

Molar Gas Volume = 24 dm3 (or 24,000 cm3)

Moles of Gas = Volume (dm3) / 24

5. Concentration of Solutions

Concentration is the amount of solute dissolved in a unit volume of solvent. It is usually measured in mol/dm3.

Conc (mol/dm3) = Moles / Volume (dm3)
Exam Tip: Unit Conversion
Volumes in questions are often given in cm3. Always convert to dm3 by dividing by 1000 before using the concentration formula.
500 cm3 = 0.5 dm3

6. Empirical and Molecular Formulae

  • Empirical Formula: The simplest whole number ratio of atoms in a compound.
  • Molecular Formula: The actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.

Calculating Empirical Formula

Follow these steps using a table:

  1. Write element symbols.
  2. Write down the mass (or %) of each.
  3. Divide mass by Ar (to get moles).
  4. Divide all answers by the smallest number of moles.
  5. If result is not whole (e.g., 1.5), multiply all by 2.

7. Percentage Yield and Purity

Reactions rarely produce 100% of the expected product due to impurities or side reactions.

% Yield = (Actual Yield / Theoretical Yield) × 100 % Purity = (Mass of pure substance / Total mass) × 100
