Chemical Energetics

Every chemical reaction involves an energy change. This chapter deals with heat energy transfers, bond energies, and the use of fuels.

1. Exothermic & Endothermic Reactions

Chemical reactions can be classified by heat transfer with the surroundings.

FeatureExothermic Endothermic
DefinitionHeat energy is released to surroundings.Heat energy is absorbed from surroundings.
TemperatureTemperature of surroundings rises.Temperature of surroundings falls.
Energy ContentProducts have less energy than reactants.Products have more energy than reactants.
Enthalpy (ΔH)Negative (-ve)Positive (+ve)
ExamplesCombustion, Neutralization, Respiration.Thermal Decomposition, Photosynthesis.

3. Bond Energies

A chemical reaction involves two steps: Breaking bonds in reactants and making bonds in products.

The Golden Rules:
1. Bond Breaking requires energy → Endothermic (+)
2. Bond Making releases energy → Exothermic (-)

ΔH = Σ(Energy to Break Bonds) - Σ(Energy Released Making Bonds)

Example Calculation: Combustion of Hydrogen

Equation: 2H-H + O=O → 2H-O-H

  • Step 1 (Break): 2 × (H-H) + 1 × (O=O) = 2(436) + 498 = 1370 kJ (In)
  • Step 2 (Make): 4 × (O-H) = 4(464) = 1856 kJ (Out)
  • Step 3 (Total): 1370 - 1856 = -486 kJ/mol

Note: Since result is negative, reaction is Exothermic.

4. Fuels & Hydrogen Fuel Cells

A fuel is a substance that releases energy when burned. Commonly used fuels are Coal, Petroleum, and Natural Gas (Methane).

Hydrogen Fuel Cell

Uses hydrogen and oxygen to produce electricity directly. It does not burn the hydrogen in the traditional sense.

Reaction in Fuel Cell:
Hydrogen + Oxygen → Water
2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
Advantages of Hydrogen FuelDisadvantages
• Product is water (No CO2/Pollution).
• Renewable if made from water solar/wind.
• Releases a lot of energy per kg.		 • Hydrogen is difficult to store (gas occupies volume).
• Explosive/Flammable.
• Currently expensive to produce H2.
Exam Watch: Remember that in a fuel cell, chemical energy is converted to electrical energy (and heat), whereas combustion converts chemical to heat energy.

5. Radioactive Isotopes (Brief)

Uranium-235 is used as a source of energy in nuclear power stations. It yields energy through Nuclear Fission, not chemical combustion.
