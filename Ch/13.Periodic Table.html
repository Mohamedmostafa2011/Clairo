The Periodic Table

The Periodic Table classifies elements by proton number. It allows us to predict properties based on an element's position. We focus on the trends in Group I, Group VII, and the Transition Elements.

1. Structure of the Table

The table is arranged in order of increasing proton number (atomic number).

  • Groups (Vertical): Elements have the same number of outer shell electrons. They have similar chemical properties.
  • Periods (Horizontal): Elements have the same number of electron shells. The character changes from metallic to non-metallic across a period (left to right).

2. Group I: Alkali Metals

Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), Potassium (K). These are soft metals stored under oil to prevent reaction with air/moisture.

PropertyTrend Down the Group (Li → K)
Melting PointDecreases (Li has highest, K has lowest)
DensityIncreases (Li is lightest)
ReactivityIncreases (K is more reactive than Li)

Reactions with Water

Group I metals react vigorously with water to produce a metal hydroxide (alkaline solution) and hydrogen gas.

General Equation:
$2M_{(s)} + 2H_2O_{(l)} \rightarrow 2MOH_{(aq)} + H_{2(g)}$

Observations:
  • Lithium: Fizzes steadily, floats, disappears.
  • Sodium: Fizzes rapidly, melts into a ball, floats.
  • Potassium: Fizzes violently, catches fire with a lilac flame.

3. Group VII: The Halogens

Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine. These are diatomic non-metals ($Cl_2$, $Br_2$).

ElementState at RTPColor
Fluorine ($F_2$)GasPale Yellow
Chlorine ($Cl_2$)GasPale Green
Bromine ($Br_2$)LiquidRed-Brown (Orange vapor)
Iodine ($I_2$)SolidGrey-Black (Purple vapor)

Trends and Reactivity

  • Melting/Boiling Point: Increases down the group.
  • Color: Get darker down the group.
  • Reactivity: Decreases down the group. (Fluorine is most reactive).

Displacement Reactions

A more reactive halogen will displace a less reactive halogen from its halide solution.

  • Chlorine + Potassium Bromide: Turns orange ($Br_2$ formed).
    $Cl_2 + 2KBr \rightarrow 2KCl + Br_2$
  • Chlorine + Potassium Iodide: Turns brown ($I_2$ formed).
  • Bromine + Potassium Chloride: No reaction (Bromine is less reactive than Chlorine).

4. Transition Elements

A block of metals between Groups II and III. They are typical metals (high density, high melting point) but have unique chemical properties.

Distinctive Properties of Transition Elements:
  1. Variable Oxidation States: Iron can form $Fe^{2+}$ and $Fe^{3+}$ ions. Copper can form $Cu^+$ and $Cu^{2+}$.
  2. Colored Compounds:
    • $Cu^{2+}$ compounds are Blue.
    • $Fe^{2+}$ compounds are Pale Green.
    • $Fe^{3+}$ compounds are Red-Brown.
  3. Catalytic Activity: They act as catalysts (e.g., Iron in Haber process, Nickel in hydrogenation).
Comparison Tip: Group I metals are soft with low MP/density. Transition metals are hard with high MP/density (except Mercury).

5. Group VIII/0: Noble Gases

Helium, Neon, Argon. They are monoatomic gases.

  • Reactivity: Extremely unreactive (Inert).
  • Reason: They have a full outer shell of electrons (stable electronic configuration). They do not need to lose, gain, or share electrons.
  • Uses: Helium for balloons (low density), Argon for light bulbs (inert atmosphere).
