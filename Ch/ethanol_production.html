🔬 Fermentation — Deep Dive
🫁
Core Concept
Why Anaerobic Respiration?

Yeast is a living organism — it respires to get energy. But depending on whether oxygen is present or not, yeast does a completely different type of respiration — and only one of them produces ethanol.

☀️ Aerobic Respiration
Oxygen present
Products: CO₂ + Water
❌ No ethanol produced
🌑 Anaerobic Respiration
Oxygen absent
Products: CO₂ + Ethanol
✅ Ethanol is produced!
💡 Key takeaway: The fermentation vessel must be sealed — no oxygen allowed in. If oxygen enters, yeast switches to aerobic respiration and makes water instead of ethanol. That's why wine and beer are fermented in airtight containers.
🍄
Biological Catalyst
Why Yeast Specifically?

Yeast is a fungus that contains special proteins called enzymes — biological catalysts that speed up the breakdown of glucose into ethanol and CO₂. Without them, the reaction is far too slow to be practical.

⚡
Speed Up
Yeast enzymes dramatically accelerate the reaction rate
🌡️
Optimal Temp
Works best at ~37°C — yeast's ideal body temperature
💀
Alcohol Limit
Yeast dies when ethanol exceeds ~15% — limits alcohol content
💡 Why this matters for distillation: Because yeast dies at ~15% ethanol, fermentation alone cannot produce pure ethanol. To get higher concentrations (like in spirits), the mixture must be distilled afterwards.
🔁
Process Breakdown
How Fermentation Works — Step by Step
1
Sugar dissolved in water
Natural sugars (C₆H₁₂O₆) from plants — grapes, barley, sugarcane — are extracted and dissolved. This is the reactant yeast will feed on.
2
Yeast added — oxygen excluded
Yeast is added and the container is sealed. Removing oxygen forces yeast into anaerobic respiration — the only pathway that produces ethanol.
3
Enzymes break down glucose
Yeast enzymes catalyse the breakdown of C₆H₁₂O₆ molecules — breaking bonds and rearranging atoms into smaller molecules at ~37°C.
4
Ethanol + CO₂ produced
Ethanol accumulates in the liquid. CO₂ bubbles out. The reaction continues until ethanol concentration reaches ~15%, at which point the ethanol kills the yeast and fermentation stops.
5
Distillation (if needed)
To increase ethanol concentration beyond 15%, the mixture is distilled — separating ethanol (lower boiling point) from water and other impurities.
🔬 Hydration of Ethene — Deep Dive
🔗
Reaction Type
Why Is This an Addition Reaction?

An addition reaction occurs when two molecules combine to form a single larger molecule — nothing is lost. In hydration, the C=C double bond in ethene opens up to let water attach.

H₂C=CH₂
Ethene
+ steam
H—O—H
Water
→ catalyst
CH₃—CH₂OH
Ethanol
💔 Bond Breaking
The C=C double bond in ethene breaks open — one bond remains, one becomes available for water to attach.
💚 Bond Forming
Water's H attaches to one C, while —OH attaches to the other. No atoms are lost — 100% atom economy.
💡 Contrast with fermentation: Fermentation produces CO₂ as a by-product — atoms are "wasted." Hydration has no by-products, making it more atom-efficient. This is why hydration is preferred for industrial ethanol production.
⚙️
Industrial Conditions
Why High Temperature & Pressure?

Unlike fermentation's gentle ~37°C, hydration requires harsh conditions. Why? Because getting water to react with ethene is difficult — the molecules need to be "forced" together.

🌡️
300°C
Temperature
📊
60–70 atm
Pressure
🧪
H₃PO₄
Catalyst
🔥
High Temperature
Gives molecules enough kinetic energy to overcome the activation energy barrier and react.
🎯
High Pressure
Forces more ethene and steam molecules together — increasing collision frequency and yield.
⚡
Phosphoric Acid
Solid catalyst (H₃PO₄ on silica) provides a surface for the reaction — lowers activation energy.
💡 Exam trap: Students often forget to mention pressure. Both temperature AND pressure are essential — the reaction simply won't work efficiently without them. Always write: "High temperature and high pressure" for full marks.
↔️
Reversible Reaction
Why Isn't It 100% Yield?

The hydration of ethene is reversible — ethanol can decompose back into ethene and water. This means the reaction never goes to completion.

C₂H₄ + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅OH
⬅️ Reverse Reaction
Some ethanol molecules break apart back into ethene and water — reducing overall yield.
🔄 Solution
Recycle unreacted ethene — pass it through the reactor again to increase overall conversion.
💡 Key insight: Even though a single pass gives only ~5–10% yield, recycling unreacted ethene multiple times brings the overall yield to ~95%. This is a key industrial optimization technique.
🛢️
Raw Material
Where Does Ethene Come From?

Ethene isn't found in nature — it's produced from crude oil, a fossil fuel. This is the main environmental disadvantage of hydration compared to fermentation.

1
Crude oil extracted
Fossil fuel pumped from underground reservoirs — non-renewable resource.
2
Fractional distillation
Crude oil heated and separated into fractions by boiling point. Naphtha fraction contains useful hydrocarbons.
3
Cracking produces ethene
Long hydrocarbon chains are cracked (broken) into smaller molecules — including ethene (C₂H₄).
💡 Exam evaluation point: The main disadvantage of hydration is its dependence on crude oil — a finite, non-renewable resource. As oil supplies dwindle, this method becomes less sustainable. Fermentation uses renewable plant sugars.
🏭
Industrial Process
How Hydration Works — Step by Step
1
Ethene and steam mixed
Pure ethene gas is mixed with steam (water vapour) in precise ratios. Both must be very pure to avoid side reactions.
2
Passed over catalyst at 300°C
The gas mixture flows over a solid catalyst — phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) supported on silica pellets — inside a reactor vessel.
3
High pressure forces reaction
At 60–70 atmospheres of pressure, molecules are forced together. The C=C bond opens and water adds across it.
4
Products separated
The output is cooled. Ethanol (liquid) condenses and is collected. Unreacted ethene gas is separated and recycled.
5
Pure ethanol collected
The ethanol is very pure — no need for distillation to remove by-products. Only traces of water need to be removed for absolute ethanol.

🔑 Key Points to Remember
🍄

Yeast is a catalyst

Without yeast, fermentation happens too slowly. Yeast speeds up the breakdown of sugar into ethanol + CO₂.

🛢️

Ethene = crude oil

Ethene is a hydrocarbon obtained from crude oil — a non-renewable resource, which is a key disadvantage.

💧

Hydration = addition

The C=C double bond in ethene breaks open and water adds on. This is an addition reaction.

♻️

Sustainability matters

Fermentation uses renewable sugar; hydration uses non-renewable crude oil. A key exam evaluation point.
