🧪 Core Foundation: Collision Theory

Before understanding the factors that affect rate, you must understand Collision Theory. For a reaction to happen, two conditions must be met:

1. Activation Energy

Particles must collide with a minimum amount of energy. If they have less than this activation energy, they simply bounce apart.

2. Frequency

The rate depends on the frequency of collisions (how often particles hit each other).

Exam Tip: Whenever explaining a factor, always link it back to changes in the energy of particles or the frequency of collisions.

🔥

Temperature

When temperature increases, particles gain more kinetic energy and move faster.

  • Collisions happen more frequently.
  • Collisions happen with more energy.

Result: A higher proportion of collisions exceed the activation energy, leading to more successful collisions per second.

💧

Concentration & Pressure

These refer to how many particles exist per unit volume.

  • Concentration: For solutions.
  • Pressure: For gases.

Result: More particles in the same space means they are closer together, causing the frequency of collisions to increase.

🪨

Surface Area

Applies to solids. Using powder instead of a block increases the surface area to volume ratio.

  • More solid particles are exposed to the reactant.
  • More points of contact are available.

Result: The frequency of collisions increases significantly.

⚡

Catalysts

A substance that speeds up a reaction but is not used up.

  • Provides an alternative reaction pathway.
  • This pathway has a lower activation energy.

Result: A greater proportion of particles now have enough energy to react, increasing the rate of successful collisions.

📝 Reaction Profiles & Examples

Common Catalysts

  • Transition metals: Cobalt, Nickel.
  • Enzymes: Biological catalysts in living organisms.

Reaction Profile

On an energy level diagram, a catalyst is shown as a curve with a lower peak than the uncatalysed reaction, representing the reduced activation energy.
