Step 2 & 3 The Golden Rules

If there is an ACID involved, choose the correct rule based on the partner.

MASH

Metal + Acid ➝ Salt + Hydrogen

Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) ➝ MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)

Why? 🧠

Acids contain H⁺ ions. Metals are usually more reactive than hydrogen. The metal "kicks out" the hydrogen to take its place in the salt.

The Mechanism ⚙️

Redox Reaction: The metal loses electrons (oxidation), and the H⁺ ions gain electrons (reduction) to become H₂ gas.

BAWS

Base + Acid ➝ Water + Salt

NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) ➝ NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Why? 🧠

This is Neutralisation. The Acid is a Proton Donor (H⁺). The Base is a Proton Acceptor (OH⁻ or O²⁻). They cancel each other out.

The Mechanism ⚙️

The net ionic equation is always:
H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) ➝ H₂O(l)
The salt is just the "spectator ions" left over.

CAWCS

Carbonate + Acid ➝ Water + CO₂ + Salt

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) ➝ CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)

Why? 🧠

Carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻) react with H⁺ to form Carbonic Acid (H₂CO₃). This is very unstable and immediately breaks apart.

The Mechanism ⚙️

1. 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ ➝ H₂CO₃
2. H₂CO₃ ➝ H₂O + CO₂
Observation: You will always see effervescence (fizzing/bubbles).

Step 4 What if there is NO Acid?

If there's no acid, don't use MASH/BAWS. Look at the oxides.

Non-Metal Oxides (Acidic)

Oxides like CO₂, SO₂, NO₂ are Acidic.

They react with Bases to form salts.

Example: CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ ➝ CaCO₃ + H₂O (Limewater test)

Metal Oxides (Basic)

Oxides like CuO, MgO, CaO are Basic.

They react with Acids (BAWS rule).

Amphoteric Oxides (The tricky ones)

Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃) and Zinc Oxide (ZnO) are special.

They can act as BOTH an acid and a base.

  • With HCl (acid) they act like a base.
  • With NaOH (base) they act like an acid (forming aluminates/zincates).
Why doesn't dry HCl gas act like an acid?

Dissociation is key.

Acids are covalent molecules (HCl). They only split into ions (H⁺ and Cl⁻) when water pulls them apart. Without water, there are no H⁺ ions, so no acidic properties.
