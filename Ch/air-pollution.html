01 — Combustion

Two Types of Combustion

When hydrocarbons in fossil fuels burn, the amount of oxygen available determines what products are formed.

🔥

Complete Combustion

Occurs when there is plenty of oxygen available. All the carbon and hydrogen in the fuel is fully oxidised, producing only harmless products.

Hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
💨

Incomplete Combustion

Occurs when oxygen is limited. The fuel is only partially burned, producing a range of harmful and toxic pollutants instead.

Hydrocarbon + O₂ → Soot + CO + CO₂ + H₂O
02 — Pollutants

Products of Incomplete Combustion

Two major pollutants are directly released when fossil fuels don't fully combust.

⚠ Respiratory Hazard
Soot
Particulates / Carbon Particles

Tiny solid carbon particles small enough to be inhaled deep into the lungs, where they lodge and cause serious respiratory damage over time.

They also contribute to global dimming — forming dark clouds in the atmosphere that reflect sunlight away from the Earth's surface.

☠ Potentially Fatal
CO
Carbon Monoxide

A colourless and odourless gas — completely undetectable by human senses, making it especially dangerous.

It binds to haemoglobin in the blood far more strongly than oxygen, blocking oxygen transport to the brain. This can cause fainting, coma, or death.

03 — Atmospheric

Other Atmospheric Pollutants

Beyond incomplete combustion, high-temperature burning and impurities in fuel produce two more dangerous gases.

Source → Sulfur Impurities
SO₂

Sulfur dioxide forms when sulfur impurities naturally found inside fossil fuels are oxidised during combustion. The sulfur reacts with oxygen in the air to produce SO₂.

Source → High Temperatures
NOₓ

Nitrogen oxides form when the extreme heat inside internal combustion engines (like car engines) forces nitrogen and oxygen from the air to react with each other — something that doesn't happen at normal temperatures.

04 — Environmental Impact

Health & Environmental Damage

SO₂ and NOₓ cause harm both directly when inhaled and indirectly through the formation of acid rain.

🫁

Respiratory Health Problems

Both sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) irritate and damage the airways when inhaled, causing breathing difficulties and aggravating conditions like asthma.

🌧️

Formation of Acid Rain

SO₂ and NOₓ dissolve in water droplets in clouds, reacting to form dilute sulfuric acid and dilute nitric acid. This acidic water then falls to the ground as acid rain, causing widespread damage:

  • Corrodes and weakens metal structures such as bridges and railings
  • Erodes and damages stone buildings, statues, and monuments
  • Kills plants and trees by stripping nutrients directly from leaves
  • Makes soil excessively acidic, preventing plant growth and devastating ecosystems
