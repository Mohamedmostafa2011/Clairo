Level 1 — Foundations

What is a Lens?

A lens is a transparent object with curved surfaces that refracts (bends) light. They form the heart of cameras, eyes, microscopes, and telescopes.

🔵

Convex (Converging) Lens

Thicker at the middle than the edges. It bends parallel rays towards a focal point. Also called a converging lensIt brings light rays together at one point.

🔴

Concave (Diverging) Lens

Thicker at the edges than the middle. It bends parallel rays away from each other. Also called a diverging lensIt spreads light rays apart from each other.

📐 Key Terms

⚫

Optical Centre (P)

The exact centre of the lens. Rays passing through this point travel in a straight line without bending.

➖

Principal Axis

An imaginary horizontal line passing through the optical centre, perpendicular to the lens surface.

✦

Principal Focus (F)

The point where parallel rays converge (convex) or appear to diverge from (concave) after refraction.

📏

Focal Length (f)

The distance between the optical centre and the principal focus. A shorter focal length = stronger lens.

🪞

Real Image

Formed where rays actually meet. Can be projected onto a screen. Always inverted for a single lens.

👁

Virtual Image

Formed where rays appear to come from (rays don't actually meet there). Cannot be projected on screen. Always upright.

Level 2 — Convex Lens

Converging Lens — Ray Diagrams

Three rules govern how we draw rays through a convex lens. Master these and you can find any image.

1️⃣

Ray Parallel to Axis

A ray travelling parallel to the principal axis refracts and passes through the focal point F on the other side.

2️⃣

Ray Through Centre

A ray passing through the optical centre (P) travels in a straight line — it is not refracted at all.

3️⃣

Ray Through Focus

A ray passing through the focal point F (on the object side) refracts and emerges parallel to the principal axis.

Level 2 — Concave Lens

Diverging Lens — Deep Dive

The concave (diverging) lens always produces the same type of image. Understanding this completely is critical for CIE exams.

A concave lens ALWAYS produces an image that is:

  • ✅ Virtual — rays don't actually meet; they diverge
  • ✅ Upright — same orientation as the object
  • ✅ Diminished — smaller than the object
  • ✅ Same side as the object — image is on the same side as where light comes from

This is true regardless of where the object is placed. The object position only changes HOW MUCH it's diminished.

📐 Ray Rules for Concave Lens

1️⃣

Ray Parallel to Axis

After refraction, the ray diverges as if it came from the focal point F on the same side as the object. Trace back to find the virtual focus.

2️⃣

Ray Through Centre

Passes straight through the optical centre without bending — same rule as convex. This ray is your anchor.

3️⃣

Ray Aimed at Far Focus

A ray directed at the far focal point refracts and travels parallel to the principal axis after leaving the lens.

🔍 Why is the concave image always virtual?

When light hits a concave lens, it spreads out (diverges). The refracted rays never actually cross on the other side. If you trace them backwards (following dashed lines), they appear to come from a point on the same side as the object. Since no rays actually pass through this point, the image is virtual and cannot be projected on a screen.

Virtual image = rays traced backwards (dashed lines)

Level 3 — Advanced Optics

Magnification & Lens Equation

Go beyond diagrams with the mathematical tools that describe image formation precisely.

📐 Magnification Formula

Magnification tells us how many times bigger the image is compared to the object.

m = image height / object height
m = image distance (v) / object distance (u)

If m > 1 → magnified  |  m < 1 → diminished  |  m = 1 → same size
If m is negative → image is inverted (real image)

🔢 Thin Lens Equation

Relates object distance (u), image distance (v), and focal length (f).

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

f = focal length (+ for convex, − for concave)
u = object distance (always positive)
v = image distance (+ if real, − if virtual)

🌍 Real-World Applications

📷

Camera

Uses a convex lens. Object is beyond 2F → real, inverted, diminished image on sensor/film. The lens can move to focus.

👁

Human Eye

The eye lens is a convex lens whose shape can change (accommodation). It focuses light onto the retina — a real, inverted image.

🔍

Magnifying Glass

Convex lens with object between F and lens. Produces a virtual, upright, magnified image. Used for reading fine print.

📽

Projector

Convex lens with object between F and 2F. Creates a real, inverted, magnified image on a distant screen.

👓

Spectacles (Short-sight)

Concave lens used to correct myopia. It diverges light before it hits the eye, moving the focus back to the retina.

🔭

Telescope

Uses two convex lenses: an objective (large) and an eyepiece (small) to magnify distant objects.
